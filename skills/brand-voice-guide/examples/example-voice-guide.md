# Brightwell Analytics LinkedIn Voice Guide

Version 1.0, 02 MAR 26. Worked example for a fictional company. Brightwell Analytics, its people and its posts are invented.

## 1. Purpose and how to use this guide

This guide sets the tone, vocabulary and standards for Brightwell's LinkedIn company page. The drafting agent loads it before every post and the reviewer uses section 11 before approving. It is built from the Brightwell website, the Brightwell guide "Energy Reporting Without the Spreadsheet" and the last 12 company posts. Scope: LinkedIn only. Spelling: UK English.

## 2. Positioning in one paragraph

**For facilities managers in mid-sized commercial buildings who have to report energy use but have no analyst, Brightwell is the reporting tool that turns existing meter data into a board-ready energy report in under an hour.** It connects to the meters and building management systems already installed, so there is no new hardware. It is built by two former facilities managers and a data engineer who spent ten years producing these reports by hand.

**The core belief behind every post:** energy reporting should take an hour, not a week, and the data to do it is already in the building.

## 3. Who we are writing for

| Audience | What they care about | What we lead with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Facilities managers | Time, accuracy, not being blamed for bad numbers | Hours saved, uses existing meters, reports a board can read |
| Sustainability leads | Credible figures for disclosures | Audit trail, methodology stated in every report |
| Building owners and finance | Cost, risk, compliance deadlines | Cost visibility by floor and tenant, no hardware spend |

## 4. Voice traits

Brightwell sounds like an experienced facilities manager explaining a shortcut to a colleague: practical, specific and a little relieved.

| Trait | What it means | Sounds like | Not like |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Been there** | We write from the plant room, not the boardroom. | "Most energy reports are built at 22:00 the night before the board meeting." | "We are transforming sustainability." |
| **Numbers with workings** | Every figure shows where it came from. | "One site cut reporting time from six days to 50 minutes (customer-reported, Q4 25)." | "Huge time savings." |
| **Plain English** | No sustainability jargon unless the reader uses it daily. | "Which floor uses the most power after hours." | "Holistic decarbonisation insights." |
| **Calm about compliance** | Deadlines are real but we do not sell fear. | "The reporting deadline is in June. Here is what the report needs to contain." | "Are you ready for the coming crackdown?" |

## 5. Content themes

| Theme | Share of posts | Example angles |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Practical how-to | 40% | Reading a half-hourly meter file, spotting after-hours load |
| Point of view | 25% | Why reporting belongs with facilities, not consultants |
| Product truth | 20% | Meter connections, report templates, audit trail |
| Company moments | 15% | Events, new integrations, team |

## 6. Post mechanics

- **Opening line:** a specific situation the reader recognises, under 15 words.
- **Length:** 60 to 180 words.
- **Structure:** one idea, short paragraphs, bullets only for steps.
- **Call to action:** one question or one invitation.
- **Links:** first comment.
- **Hashtags:** three, always #Brightwell and #EnergyReporting.
- **Style:** UK spelling, metric units (kWh, m²), no emoji, no exclamation marks.
- **Names:** "Brightwell" alone, never "Brightwell Analytics Ltd" in posts.
- **Person:** "we" and "you".

## 7. On-tone examples

Examples 1 and 2 are published posts. Example 3 is a new draft.

### Example 1: Practical how-to (published, JAN 26)

> Most energy reports are built at 22:00 the night before the board meeting.
>
> The data was there all along. Half-hourly meter files, already sitting in the building management system.
>
> Three things to pull before you open a spreadsheet:
> Baseload between 00:00 and 05:00
> Weekday versus weekend use
> The five highest half-hours of the month
>
> What is the first number you check each month?
>
> #Brightwell #EnergyReporting #FacilitiesManagement

**Why it works:** a scene every facilities manager recognises, three usable steps, and a question that invites experience.

### Example 2: Point of view (published, FEB 26)

> Energy reporting keeps being handed to consultants. It belongs with the people who know which chiller was off for maintenance in March.
>
> Context is what turns a number into an explanation. Facilities teams have it. They just do not have the hours.
>
> That is the problem we built Brightwell to solve.
>
> #Brightwell #EnergyReporting #Facilities

**Why it works:** a clear opinion, grounded in a specific detail, with the product mentioned once and late.

### Example 3: Product truth (new draft)

> No new meters. No site visit. No hardware budget.
>
> Brightwell reads the meter and BMS data your building already produces. Most sites are connected in a day.
>
> The first report shows consumption by floor, after-hours load and month-on-month change, with the method written out on the last page so your auditor can follow it.
>
> Facilities managers: what does your current report take to produce?
>
> #Brightwell #EnergyReporting #FacilitiesManagement

**Why it works:** leads with the objection the buyer has (cost and disruption) and answers it with specifics.

## 8. Off-tone examples

Invented examples.

### Off-tone 1: Generic hype

> 🌍 Big news! Brightwell is revolutionising sustainability with AI-powered insights that unlock net zero for every business! The future of energy is here! #Sustainability #AI #NetZero

**Why it fails:**

- No reader, no situation, no number.
- "Unlock net zero" is a claim Brightwell cannot support.
- Emoji and exclamation marks break the house style.

### Off-tone 2: Fear and overclaim

> New regulations are coming and most companies will be fined. Brightwell guarantees 100% compliance. Do not get caught out. Book a demo now.

**Why it fails:**

- Sells fear, which contradicts the "calm about compliance" trait.
- "Guarantees 100% compliance" is a legal claim the company cannot make.
- Hard-sell call to action.

### Drift to watch in published posts

- "We're excited to announce" openers on three of the last 12 posts.
- Two posts used "kilowatt hours" and "kWh" in the same post.

## 9. Words to use and words to avoid

| Use | Avoid | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| energy report, energy reporting | sustainability insights, ESG intelligence | The buyer's own words |
| meter data, half-hourly data | big data, AI-powered | Specific and true |
| facilities team, facilities manager | users, stakeholders | Names the reader |
| hours saved (with source) | effortless, seamless | Evidence over adjectives |
| reporting deadline | crackdown, fines are coming | Calm, not fearful |
| method, audit trail | proprietary algorithm | Credibility comes from transparency |

## 10. Guardrails for the drafting agent

- Draft only. A person approves every post.
- Every figure names its source and date.
- No customer names without written permission.
- Never claim compliance outcomes. Describe what the report contains.
- Never criticise a named competitor or consultancy.

## 11. Pre-publish checklist

- Opening line is a recognisable situation in under 15 words?
- One idea and one call to action?
- Every figure sourced?
- Passes the words-to-avoid list?
- UK spelling, kWh and m² used consistently?
- Three hashtags?
- Proofread?

## 12. Open items to confirm

- Confirm whether "under an hour" can be stated as a general claim or only as a customer-reported example.
- Confirm the approved name for the reporting template ("Board Report" or "Monthly Energy Report").
